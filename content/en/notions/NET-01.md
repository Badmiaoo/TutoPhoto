# NET-01 · Depth of field

## Explanation

A lens focuses at only one distance; the further an object is from the plane of focus, the more its image becomes a blur spot. Depth of field is the range of distances over which objects appear sharp, that is, where this spot stays below a blur judged acceptable ([Wikipedia, "Circle of confusion", revision 1335590423](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Circle_of_confusion&oldid=1335590423), read on 11 November 2026). For full frame, a widely used limit is the sensor diagonal divided by 1500, or 0.029 mm; the most appropriate value depends on visual acuity, viewing conditions and enlargement (same source).

The four levers can be read in the sharp-zone formula (q = c × N × (S − f) / f², limits S / (1 + q) and S / (1 − q)): stopping down (larger N), focusing further away (S), using a shorter focal length (f), or accepting a larger blur (c, photo viewed smaller). Examples at 50 mm, focused at 6.6 m: from 4.1 m to 16.6 m at f/8, from 6.0 m to 7.4 m at f/1.4; at 135 mm and f/4, from 6.3 m to 6.9 m.

## Simulator

Depth of field shows in the viewfinder (adjustable preview), on the computed photo, and in the top view, which draws the sharp zone between two planes perpendicular to the axis and circles in green the subjects inside it. The balance indicator and the report give the limits in metres.

## Exercise

Blur the background without changing the aperture. At f/4 and 50 mm, focus on Sophia's eye: the sharp zone goes from 5.1 m to 9.5 m, and the dog running behind her may be in it. Without touching the aperture, go to 135 mm: the sharp zone shrinks to 6.3–6.9 m. Pass challenge SIT-01 this way.
