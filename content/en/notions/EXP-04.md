# EXP-04 · Aperture

## Explanation

The f-number (N) is the ratio of the lens's focal length to the diameter of its entrance pupil ([Wikipedia, "F-number", revision 1382682702](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=F-number&oldid=1382682702), read on 11 November 2026). The larger it is, the darker the image projected on the sensor: illuminance decreases with the square of the f-number (same source).

From one full stop to the next (f/1.4, f/2, f/2.8, f/4, f/5.6, f/8, f/11, f/16, f/22), the diameter is multiplied by 1/√2, about 0.71, and the area that lets light through is halved: one stop is one EV (same source). Cameras also offer third stops (f/3.2, f/3.5, then f/4…), which TutoPhoto uses.

Aperture also matters for depth of field (same source): see NET-01.

## Simulator

The "Aperture" dial goes from f/1.4 to f/22 in third stops. The meter and the viewfinder preview show the effect on exposure at once; the top view and the viewfinder show the effect on the sharp zone. In mode A, you choose the aperture and the camera sets the shutter speed; in mode S, the camera chooses it.

## Exercise

In mode M, without auto ISO, set f/5.6 and a shutter speed that puts the meter at 0. Find the aperture that gives +1 EV without touching the shutter speed or the ISO. Answer: f/4, one full stop wider, since (5.6 / 4)² ≈ 1.96, about twice as much light.
