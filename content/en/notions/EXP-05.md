# EXP-05 · Shutter speed

## Explanation

Shutter speed, or exposure time, is the length of time the sensor receives light when a photo is taken ([Wikipedia, "Shutter speed", revision 1382495283](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Shutter_speed&oldid=1382495283), read on 11 November 2026). Doubling the exposure time doubles the amount of light; the scale of shutter speeds is standardised in doublings, so that one shutter-speed step offsets one aperture stop (same source).

During the exposure, whatever moves leaves a trail on the sensor, longer as the exposure is longer: see MVT-01.

## Simulator

The "Shutter speed" dial goes from 30 s to 1/4000 s in third stops. The meter shows the effect on exposure; the balance indicator and the photo report give the trail of the aimed subject in pixels. In mode S, you choose the shutter speed; in mode A, the camera sets it. In burst mode, a frame never lasts less than its exposure.

## Exercise

In mode M, without auto ISO, set f/4 and 1/400 s, then go to f/8: the meter drops by two stops (−2 EV). Get back to the starting exposure with the shutter speed alone. Answer: 1/100 s, four times longer, since f/4 → f/8 is two stops, four times less light.
