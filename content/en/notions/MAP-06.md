# MAP-06 · AF points

## Explanation

The Nikon Z 6 has 273 focus points in single-point AF, in FX format, covering approximately 90% of the image horizontally and vertically ([Nikon, press release of 23 August 2018](https://www.nikon.com/news/2018/0823_mirrorless_01.htm); [product page](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/lineup/mirrorless/z_6/), read on 10 November 2026). Outside this area, the camera cannot focus. The focus point is moved with the sub-selector or the multi selector, and setting a5 uses only a quarter of the points for quicker selection ([online manual, "Focus"](https://onlinemanual.nikonimglib.com/z7_z6/en/05_basic_settings_01.html)).

## Simulator

Tapping the viewfinder places the focus point. The area covered by autofocus is drawn as a dotted line; a tap outside it brings the point back to its edge. Coverage depends on the body. The Canon EOS RP covers 88% of the frame horizontally and 100% vertically, depending on the lens ([Canon's specifications](https://www.canon.co.uk/cameras/eos-rp/specifications/)). The Sony A7 III's phase-detection AF points cover approximately 93% of the image area ([Sony's page](https://www.sony.co.in/interchangeable-lens-cameras/products/ilce-7m3-body-kit/feature2)); Sony does not say how that area is split, and TutoPhoto splits it equally, about 96.4% horizontally and vertically, which is not sourced. Bodies whose coverage is not yet quantified keep the Z 6's. The layout of the 273 points is not published in the sources read: the point moves continuously, without a grid. The selectors and setting a5 are not simulated.

## Exercise

The reference sheet suggests focusing on the nearest eye at f/1.8, subject off-centre. At 135 mm, frame Sophia on the side of the frame, set f/1.8 in mode A, single-point AF, and tap her eye: pass challenge SIT-01. Calculation: at 135 mm, f/1.8 and 6.6 m, the sharp zone goes from 6.48 m to 6.72 m, about 24 cm. Then tap a point further behind her and look at the report: the eye leaves the sharp zone.
