# MAP-04 · Single AF, continuous AF

## Explanation

The Nikon Z 6 manual distinguishes two autofocus modes ([Nikon, Z 7/Z 6 online manual, "Focus"](https://onlinemanual.nikonimglib.com/z7_z6/en/05_basic_settings_01.html), read on 9 November 2026):
- **AF-S**, for stationary subjects: focus is acquired with the shutter-release button pressed halfway and "focus will lock while the shutter-release button is pressed halfway"; by default, the camera only releases the shutter once focus is acquired;
- **AF-C**, for moving subjects: the camera focuses continuously while the button is pressed halfway, and predicts the distance of a moving subject; by default, it releases the shutter even without focus.

The Sony α9 describes the same modes ([Sony, α9 help guide, "Focus Mode"](https://helpguide.sony.net/ilc/1650/v1/en/contents/TP0001486782.html)). AF-S is the Z 6 default (["Defaults"](https://onlinemanual.nikonimglib.com/z7_z6/en/09_menu_guide_01.html)).

## Simulator

"Focus" window: AF-S or AF-C. A touch screen has no half-press: tapping the viewfinder stands in for it. In AF-S, the tap focuses and locks until the next tap; in AF-C, the distance is measured again five times per second. Focusing time, focusing failures and motion prediction are not simulated: in AF-C, focus at release is exact.

## Exercise

The reference sheet suggests a sharp burst of a dog running towards you; in the park, the dog runs sideways, about 9 m away. In AF-S, lock focus on the group (6.5 m), then photograph the dog as it passes: the report shows it outside the sharp zone at f/2. Try again in AF-C, auto-area AF with animal detection, in Continuous H: challenge SIT-06 can pass.
