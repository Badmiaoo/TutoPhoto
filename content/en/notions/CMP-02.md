# CMP-02 · Rule of thirds, symmetry

## Explanation

Several cameras can display a grid to help with framing. On the Nikon Z 6, setting d9 displays "a grid for reference when framing shots"; it is off by default ([Nikon, Z 7/Z 6 online manual, "d9: Framing Grid Display"](https://onlinemanual.nikonimglib.com/z7_z6/en/09_menu_guide_05_d09.html); ["Defaults"](https://onlinemanual.nikonimglib.com/z7_z6/en/09_menu_guide_01.html), read on 12 November 2026). On the Sony A7 III, the "Grid Line" setting offers a "Rule of 3rds Grid": "Place main subjects close to one of the grid lines that divide the image into thirds for a well-balanced composition" ([Sony, ILCE-7M3 Help Guide, "Grid Line"](https://helpguide.sony.net/ilc/1720/v1/en/contents/TP0001653165.html)). The Sony A6000 has the same setting, with the same text, and it is off by default ([ILCE-6000 Help Guide, "Grid Line"](https://helpguide.sony.net/gbmig/45349331/v1/en/contents/TP0000518347.html)).

The rule of thirds is a rule of thumb for composition ([Wikipedia, "Rule of thirds", revision 1383322855](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rule_of_thirds&oldid=1383322855), a secondary source, read on 12 November 2026). According to that article:
- the image is imagined as divided into nine equal parts by two equally spaced horizontal lines and two equally spaced vertical lines, and important elements are placed along these lines or at their intersections;
- the theory is that a subject aligned with these points creates more tension, energy and interest than a subject simply centred; the main reason for the rule is to discourage placing the subject at the centre, or to prevent a horizon from appearing to divide the picture in half;
- with a person, it is common to line the body up with a vertical line and the eyes with a horizontal line; when a still person is not directly facing the camera, most of the extra room goes in front of them;
- the expression "rule of thirds" was first written down in 1797 by John Thomas Smith, in "Remarks on Rural Scenery": he proposes proportions of two thirds to one third, for example between the sky and the rest of a landscape; the article adds that this discussion is independent of the use of the term in composition and photography;
- it is not a law: as early as 1845, George Field doubted that such a rule was universal, at least for colours ("it is neither accurate nor universal"), and a 2014 study (Amirshahi and colleagues) concluded that the rule "seems to play only a minor, if any, role" in large sets of high-quality photographs and paintings.

**Centre and symmetry.** The article does not say when to centre the subject, and none of the pages read deals with symmetry. This part of the notion awaits a source.

## Simulator

"Preview" window, "Framing grid" group: "None" (the default), "Thirds" or "Sixteen boxes". The grid is drawn inside the viewfinder frame; tapping the viewfinder still moves the focus point.
- **Thirds**: two vertical lines and two horizontal lines, at the thirds of the frame. This layout is deduced from Sony's text for "Rule of 3rds Grid" on the A7 III and the A6000 ("grid lines that divide the image into thirds"): the page has no illustration and does not give the number of lines.
- **Sixteen boxes**: three vertical lines and three horizontal lines, at the quarters of the frame. This is what the illustration of the Nikon D5600's "Framing grid" display shows in live view ([Nikon, Digitutor D5600, "Framing Grid (Live View)"](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/support/digitutor/d5600/functions/framing_grid_liveview.html)); the text of the page does not give the number of lines.

The grid is a TutoPhoto aid, the same for every body, not a copy of each body's menu. TutoPhoto's choices, which none of the pages read describes: the grid is hidden while the magnifier enlarges the image, and it is drawn neither on the photo nor on the review screen. Not simulated: the Nikon Z 6 grid, whose layout the manual does not describe; Sony's "Square Grid" and "Diag. + Square Grid", whose number of boxes and diagonals are not described; the grids of the Canon EOS RP and EOS 2000D, whose manuals have not been read.

## Exercise

The reference sheet suggests comparing two placements and justifying your choice. In the park, with the "Thirds" grid:
- frame Sophia on a vertical thirds line, her eyes near a horizontal line, focus on her eye and release the shutter, then save the photo;
- without changing the settings, turn the camera to place her at the centre of the frame, focus on her eye again and release the shutter, then save the photo;
- compare the two photos: which one do you keep, and why? Say what the placement changes, for example the room left in front of her and what enters the frame beside her.

The report does not judge where the subject is placed: there is no computed right answer. For the settings of the portrait (sharp eyes, blurred background), see situation SIT-01.
