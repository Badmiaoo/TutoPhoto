# LUM-02 · Hard, soft light

## Explanation

A light source is soft when it appears larger than the subject it lights: it covers the subject from a greater angle and fills in more of the shadow areas ([Tiffen, Lowel guide "Foundations of Lighting: Understanding Soft Light"](https://tiffen.reamaze.com/kb/lowel-guides/foundations-of-lighting-understanding-soft-light), read on 11 November 2026). The edge of a shadow shows why: an object hides a large source gradually, so the shadow darkens progressively instead of falling all at once ([Art Adams, "Lighting Strategies: What Makes Soft Lights Cast Soft Shadows?", ProVideo Coalition, 27 December 2011](https://www.provideocoalition.com/lighting_strategies_what_makes_soft_lights_cast_soft_shadows/)).

What counts is the apparent size, seen from the subject: a large source very far away appears tiny. The sun, much larger than the subject but very far away, therefore gives hard light with sharp shadows (same sources). Under an overcast sky, the whole cloudy sky becomes the source; much larger than the subject, it lights it from a very large angle and gives very soft light. Moving a source back makes it smaller, so harder; passing it through a diffuser or bouncing it off a surface makes it larger, so softer (Tiffen).

## Activity

The light simulator is planned for V2 and the interactive activity in TutoPhoto is still to be built. Meanwhile, an activity to do at home, based on the same sources:
- put an object on a table and light it with a small lamp without a shade: look at the edge of its shadow;
- move the lamp closer, then further away: does the edge change?
- bounce the light off a large white wall or card, so that it becomes the source: the edge of the shadow softens.
With a hot bulb, never put fabric or paper against it.
