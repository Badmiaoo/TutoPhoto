# NET-06 · Subject-background distance

## Explanation

The further an object is from the plane of focus, the larger its blur spot ([Wikipedia, "Circle of confusion", revision 1335590423](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Circle_of_confusion&oldid=1335590423), read on 11 November 2026). With focus on the subject at distance S₁, a background at S₂ gives a spot of diameter c = A × |S₂ − S₁| / S₂ × f / (S₁ − f), with A = f / N (same source): the spot grows with the gap between subject and background, up to a maximum for a background at infinity.

Calculation at 50 mm and f/4, focused on Sophia at 6.6 m (A = 12.5 mm): the bin at 10.5 m gives 0.035 mm, 1.2 times the acceptable blur of 0.029 mm; the walker at 12.5 m, 0.045 mm (1.6 times); the photo background, at infinity, 0.095 mm (3.3 times).

## Simulator

The top view lets you move the camera on the ground: getting closer to the subject changes the ratio between its distance and the background's. The people do not move yet, although the reference sheet also plans to move the subject. The 360° photo background is at 500 m, which amounts to infinity.
